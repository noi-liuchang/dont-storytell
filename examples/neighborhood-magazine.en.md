# Worked example: a neighborhood magazine

[English](neighborhood-magazine.en.md) · [中文原文](neighborhood-magazine.md) · [Back to the guide](../README.md)

This is a fictional situation and a proposed strategy. It has not been implemented or approved as a final client deliverable.

## Input

> I’m starting an independent neighborhood magazine. A shopping center will cover a year’s paper costs if it gets to choose the person on every cover. Neighbors are willing to nominate people, but nobody has subscribed. We want the magazine to belong to the neighborhood. How do we make it a business?

## From the material to a judgment

- The input supports an offer of sponsorship and a willingness to nominate people. It does not establish subscriptions or dependable revenue. “Belong to the neighborhood” is an aspiration, not evidence of the founder’s life story.
- Two directions are plausible: produce a publication for the shopping center, or build a reader-supported magazine with independent editorial choices. The recommendation is to test the latter because it better matches the stated aspiration. Declining money tied to cover control is a proposed trade-off for the founder to decide.
- Useful observations include whether the sponsor accepts a separate advertising arrangement and whether readers pay for an issue about someone they do not know, then buy another. Nominations, reading, advance payment, and repeat purchase each establish different things.
- Judgment card 33 distinguishes the interests of the funder and the reader; card 13 separates agreement from commitment; card 69 examines attached conditions. None requires rejecting all sponsorship. The path grows from readers’ and editors’ participation; it does not need a famous founder’s story as a template.

## The one-page strategy

### Who chooses the first cover?

The shopping center can pay for twelve covers. The neighbors can nominate a person. I would first test a magazine readers will pay for, where someone without an advertising budget can still become the cover story. The future worth pursuing is a reader who starts by reading about a neighbor, then brings the editors someone worth meeting. Today there is sponsorship interest and a willingness to nominate people. There is no subscription evidence yet.

**First, decide who the magazine answers to.**

Keep nominations open to neighbors and story selection with the editors. Offer the shopping center clearly marked advertising space. If it will only pay for control of the cover, decline that arrangement for now. Producing its publication is a viable business, but it changes what you mean by a magazine that belongs to the neighborhood. The editorial team must decide whether to accept this trade-off.

**The first issue has to earn a second.**

Choose someone from the nominations who agrees to be interviewed. Produce a sample and offer advance copies at a stated price. Print when presales and agreed advertising cover reporting, printing, and distribution for that issue. Feature someone different in the next issue and see whether the original readers buy again. If they only buy because they know the cover subject, treat it as a standalone issue about that person and hold off on a permanent editorial team.

**Give readers a reason to bring you the next person.**

If readers return for stories about people they do not know, invite them to suggest the next subject. Editors follow up and check the facts. Give sources and nominators a chance to correct factual errors in the draft. The reporting relationships, reader trust, and repeat sales may then support another contributor. At that point, test a short subscription term, with advertising covering part of the cost and editors retaining story selection. If nominations are plentiful but repeat sales are weak, publish less often and review costs before hiring.

**Next step.**

This week, agree on editorial control within the team. Then offer the shopping center an advertising proposal and arrange the first interview. Do not book the printer before the subject has agreed to participate and real advance payments have arrived.

The future described here, the partnership terms, and each step are recommendations. The founder’s personal history is unknown; this proposal cannot establish their convictions for them.
